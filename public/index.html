Which planets are used

Radial-velocity, microlensing and imaging surveys mostly find giant planets, and every planet they find has a measured mass. Transit surveys measure radius directly, so the analysis and the later models use transit planets only. The other methods stay in the database.

Data quality

Mass is missing for most transit planets, because small planets are hard to weigh. Masses that NASA estimated from the radius were removed, since they would leak the answer into the features. Every other feature is missing for only a small share of planets.

How the two classes differ

Terrestrial planets tend to orbit closer, receive more starlight and have lower mass. The distributions overlap heavily, so no single measurement separates the classes on its own.

How the measurements relate

The scatter plot is coloured by radius, so its horizontal boundary is built in. It shows where planets sit, not how well the other measurements separate them. In the heatmap, several pairs are strongly correlated: orbital period with orbit size, starlight with temperature, and the three star properties with each other. Those features carry much of the same information.
